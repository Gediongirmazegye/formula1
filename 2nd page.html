Formula One

The pinnacle of international single-seater motor racing.

About Formula One

Formula One, commonly known as F1, is a global motorsport championship. Teams design and build advanced racing cars, while drivers compete in a series of Grand Prix events held at circuits around the world.

The championship is organized by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). Each season includes a Drivers' Championship and a Constructors' Championship.

How a race weekend works

  • Practice: Teams prepare their cars and learn the circuit.
  • Qualifying: Drivers compete for their starting positions on the grid.
  • Grand Prix: Drivers race to complete the scheduled distance as quickly as possible.

Points are awarded to the highest-finishing drivers, and teams earn points from their drivers' results.

The cars

F1 cars are highly engineered machines built for speed, cornering, and safety. They use hybrid power units that combine a turbocharged engine with energy-recovery systems. Teams also develop aerodynamic parts to manage airflow and increase grip.

What makes F1 exciting?

  1. Close competition between drivers and teams
  2. Strategy involving tires, pit stops, and changing conditions
  3. Races at a wide range of circuits and locations
  4. Continual advances in engineering and technology
